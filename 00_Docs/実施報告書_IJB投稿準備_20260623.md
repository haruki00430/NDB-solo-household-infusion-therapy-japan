## Are Heat-Health Systems Socially Blind?

**Version 1.0.0 — analysis code archive for the submitted manuscript.**

### Paper

**Title**: Are Heat-Health Systems Socially Blind? Social Isolation and Dehydration-Related Healthcare Utilization Across Japan

**Authors**: Haruki Saito, Tetsuya Ohira

**Target journal**: *International Journal of Biometeorology* (submitted, 2026)

**Study design**: Nationwide ecological study (N = 47 prefectures; fiscal year 2023)

**Primary outcome**: Prefecture-level dehydration-related healthcare utilization, operationalized as infusion therapy claims (≥500 mL; NDB procedure code G004).

**Key finding**: Elderly solo household rate was the only significant ecological predictor of infusion therapy utilization (β = 723.37; 95% CI, 376.52–1,070.21; R² = 0.282; *p* = 0.0001), whereas heatwave days, mean wet-bulb globe temperature (WBGT), and air-conditioning prevalence were not significantly associated.

### Contents

| Directory | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `03_Analysis/etl/` | ETL scripts (JMA weather data, Census 2020 elderly solo household rate) |
| `03_Analysis/analysis/` | OLS regression, ridge regression, sensitivity analyses, figure generation |
| `03_Analysis/results/` | Output figures and result tables |
| `04_Manuscripts/` | Quarto manuscript source (`.qmd`), reference library |

### Data sources

* **NDB Open Data No.10** (MHLW, FY2023) — infusion therapy rate (G004, ≥500 mL)
* **Japan Meteorological Agency** — heatwave days (≥35°C) and mean WBGT (June–September 2023)
* **National Census 2020** (Statistics Bureau of Japan) — elderly solo household rate and population
* **National Survey of Family Income and Expenditure 2014** (MIC) — air-conditioning prevalence
* **Fire and Disaster Management Agency** — heatstroke ambulance transport (external validation; Online Resource 1, panel a)
* **R5 Patient Survey** (MHLW) — general outpatient rate (negative control; Online Resource 1, panel b)

> **Note**: NDB raw data are not included in this archive and are not redistributable. Aggregate open data are available from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/0000177182.html
